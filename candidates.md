# Candidate haiku — Seventeen Syllables of Debugging

Six original candidates, each drafted and counted by hand. Scoring is
against the five published judge criteria (most important first):
1) haiku about debugging, three lines in 5-7-5; 2) concrete, fresh centre
picture; 3) the last line turns / reframes; 4) every word earns its place;
5) reads aloud cleanly, like speech.

## Candidate 1 — "The lying log"

> A breakpoint holds still
> while the log line lies to me
> typo all along

- Line 1: A(1) break-point(2) holds(1) still(1) = **5**
- Line 2: while(1) the(1) log(1) line(1) lies(1) to(1) me(1) = **7**
- Line 3: ty-po(2) all(1) a-long(2) = **5**
- Total: 68 characters.
- Scores: (1) form holds, on-topic — good. (2) breakpoint + log line are
  concrete, but "log line lies" is a familiar image — middling. (3) the
  turn is real (the liar was a typo) but line 3 is terse to the point of
  abruptness. (4) no filler. (5) reads fine, though line 3 feels clipped.

## Candidate 2 — "The midnight cursor"

> Midnight cursor blinks
> counting my failures in green
> and the bug was me

- Line 1: Mid-night(2) cur-sor(2) blinks(1) = **5**
- Line 2: count-ing(2) my(1) fail-ures(2) in(1) green(1) = **7**
- Line 3: and(1) the(1) bug(1) was(1) me(1) = **5**
- Total: 71 characters.
- Scores: (1) form holds, on-topic — good. (2) the 3 a.m. cursor is
  exactly the brief's example image, and "counting my failures in green"
  is vivid — strong. (3) "the bug was me" reframes the whole poem as
  self-blame — strong turn. (4) "and" in line 3 is doing real work
  (the confession). (5) reads cleanly, speech-like. Runner-up.

## Candidate 3 — "The timestamp alibi"

> three a.m., and still no clue

- Line 1: One(1) log(1) line(1) time-stamped(2) = **5**
- Line 2: three(1) a.m.(2) and(1) still(1) no(1) clue(1) = **7**

- Scores: (1) form holds, on-topic — good. (2) a timestamped log line

  (3) "blame the clock, not me" deflects the blame, a real turn, but

## Candidate 4 — "The duck knows"

> Rubber duck just stares
> I explain the bug aloud
> it knew all along

- Line 1: Rub-ber(2) duck(1) just(1) stares(1) = **5**
- Line 2: I(1) ex-plain(2) the(1) bug(1) a-loud(2) = **7**
- Line 3: it(1) knew(1) all(1) a-long(2) = **5**
- Total: 66 characters.
- Scores: (1) form holds, on-topic — good. (2) the rubber duck is
  concrete and instantly legible to any debugger — strong, though the
  duck is a well-worn trope. (3) "it knew all along" flips the explainer
  into the explained — strong turn. (4) every word earns its place.
  (5) reads cleanly. Runner-up; loses to the winner only on freshness
  of the centre image.

## Candidate 5 — "The family tree" ★ CHOSEN

> The stack trace points up
> each function blames its mother
> grandma wrote the bug

- Line 1: The(1) stack(1) trace(1) points(1) up(1) = **5**
- Line 2: each(1) func-tion(2) blames(1) its(1) moth-er(2) = **7**
- Line 3: grand-ma(2) wrote(1) the(1) bug(1) = **5**
- Total: 80 characters.
- Scores: (1) form holds, on-topic — good. (2) the stack trace as a
  family tree is concrete (a real debugging artefact) and fresh — the
  strongest centre image of the six. (3) the last line reframes the
  whole trace: the blame chain ends at grandma, the original author —
  a genuine turn, and the joke lands because the trace really does
  point up through callers. (4) no filler; "points up" is the trace's
  actual direction. (5) reads aloud like a spoken complaint, not a
  syllable exercise.
- Chosen because it wins on criteria 2 and 3, the two that decide most
  pairwise games after form.

## Candidate 6 — "Stage fright"

> Works here, fails in prod
> it only performs for crowds
> a shy bug, stage fright

- Line 1: Works(1) here(1) fails(1) in(1) prod(1) = **5**
- Line 2: it(1) on-ly(2) per-forms(2) for(1) crowds(1) = **7**
- Line 3: a(1) shy(1) bug(1) stage(1) fright(1) = **5**
- Total: 78 characters.
- Scores: (1) form holds, on-topic — good. (2) the production-only bug
  is a real, concrete debugging experience — good. (3) "stage fright"
  reframes the bug as a nervous performer — a good turn, though it
  restates line 2's "performs for crowds" rather than reframing it.
  (4) "a shy bug" is slightly padded. (5) reads cleanly.

## Verdict

Candidate 5 ("The family tree") is the entry. It is the only candidate
that is fresh on criterion 2 (stack trace as genealogy, not the
much-used duck or 3 a.m. cursor) while landing a full reframe on
criterion 3, with no filler and a speech-like rhythm.
